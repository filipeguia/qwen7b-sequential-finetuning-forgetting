​
BEST TRACK 
​
​
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Tropical Storm Fernand are listed in Table 
1. The “best track” chart of Fernand’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure 
histories along with available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. Observations 
also include flight-level measurements from 1 flight of the 53rd Weather Reconnaissance 
Squadron of the U.S. Air Force Reserve Command. 
​
There were no ship reports of winds of tropical storm force associated with Fernand. 
Origin 
Fernand developed from a portion of a tropical wave that moved off the west coast of 
Africa on 14–15 August.  The wave moved westward across the tropical Atlantic Ocean for 7 
days.  A few hundred miles east of the Lesser Antilles on 21–22 August, the northern portion of 
the wave turned northwestward to northward and became more convectively organized.  Around 
1800 UTC 23 August, Air Force Hurricane Hunter aircraft data indicated that the system had a 
well-defined circulation marking the formation of a tropical storm located about 465 n mi 
south-southeast of Bermuda. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Fernand’s peak intensity of 50 kt is supported by an ASCAT pass from 1343 UTC 25 
August that had a peak vector of 48 kt.  The minimum central pressure of 999 mb is primarily 
based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney pressure-wind relationship.  ​

​
FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION​
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category.  The formation of Fernand was 
generally well forecast, with the system being introduced into the 7-day TWO 162 hours before 
genesis.  Probabilities for the 2-day TWO were introduced 120 hours before development. 
Figure 4 shows composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for each category prior to the 
formation of Fernand.  The initial genesis areas did not encompass the ultimate formation 
location.  Instead they highlighted the area east of the northern Leeward Islands, suggesting 
early model guidance and official forecasts expected Fernand to form earlier than it did.  Once 
the probabilities were raised to the high category (>60%) in the 7-day TWO 66 hours before 
development, the genesis areas consistently encompassed the formation location. 
A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Fernand is given in Table 3a. Official 
track forecast errors were significantly greater than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr 
period.  The NHC errors were also greater than the climatology/persistence model (OCD5) for 
the 36–72 hour forecast periods, indicating that the official prediction lacked skill during that time 
period. The main factor contributing to the high errors was a noticeable northward bias of the 
official track prediction (not shown). A homogeneous comparison of the official track errors with 
selected guidance models is given in Table 3b.  Many of the various individual models and 
consensus aids had lower track errors, especially beyond the 24-h mark.  The Google 
DeepMind ensemble mean (GDMI) had the lowest error for most of the verifying forecast hours 
and was comparable to the best performing aids at 60 and 72 hours.  It should be noted that 
GDMI was an experimental (non-operational) aid and not always available to the forecasters in 
real-time. 
A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Fernand is given in Table 4a. Official 
intensity forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period. A 
homogeneous comparison of the official intensity errors with selected guidance models is given 
in Table 4b.  The official forecast generally had some of the lowest errors compared to the 
intensity models throughout the forecast period, except for 48 h. 
There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Fernand.
