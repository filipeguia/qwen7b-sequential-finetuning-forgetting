BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities are listed in Table 1.  The best track chart of 
Gil’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories along with available observations3 
shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. 
There were no land-based or ship reports of tropical-storm-force winds associated with 
Gil.   
Origin 
Gil formed around 0000 UTC 31 July about 650 n mi south-southwest of the southern tip 
of the Baja California peninsula.  Gil appears to have formed from a combination of a tropical 
wave that crossed Central America on 26 and 27 July and a disturbance in the monsoon trough. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Gil’s peak intensity of 65 kt from 0000 to 1200 UTC 2 August is based on T4.0/65 kt Dvorak 
classifications from both TAFB/SAB and SATCON during that time period.  The minimum central 
pressure of 991 mb is primarily based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney pressure wind relationship. 
 
CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS 
 
  
There were no reports of damage or casualties associated with Gil.

FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 lists the number of hours prior to formation corresponding to the first issuance of 
an NHC Tropical Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category.  The formation of 
Gil was generally well anticipated.  The precursor disturbance was first introduced in the 7-day 
TWO with a low probability of genesis 138 h before development. This likelihood was 
subsequently increased to the medium category 120 h in advance, and to the high category 72 h 
prior to formation.  Lead times for the 2-day outlooks demonstrated notable reliability.  A low 
probability was first indicated 72 h before genesis, with increases to medium and high probabilities 
at 60 h and 48 h prior to development, respectively. Figure 4 shows composites of the 7-day TWO 
genesis areas for each likelihood category leading up to the formation of Gil.  The anticipated 
formation region turned out to be too far east for the first few genesis predictions. 
A verification of NHC official track and intensity forecasts for Gil are given in Table 3a and 
4a, respectively.  A homogeneous comparison of the official track and intensity errors with 
selected guidance models are given in Table 3b and 4b, respectively. The mean official track 
forecast errors for Gil were excellent, and well below the long-term averages.  The only models 
that consistently outperformed NHC’s track forecasts were  EMXI and HCCA.  The NHC mean 
official intensity errors for Gil were also quite low, and well below the 5-yr averages.  Most of the 
hurricane regional models performed quite well for this system too. 
There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Gil.
