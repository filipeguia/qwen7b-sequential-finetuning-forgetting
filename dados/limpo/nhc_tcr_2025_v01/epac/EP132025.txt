Tropical Storm Mario (EP132025), 11–16 September 2025

Mario produced gusty winds and heavy rainfall when it passed just offshore of the coast of southwestern Mexico as a small tropical storm. After briefly degenerating into a trough, Mario redeveloped into a stronger tropical storm that moved over open waters well offshore of the Baja California peninsula.

Tropical Storm Mario 11–16 September 2025

## SYNOPTIC HISTORY

Mario appears to have originated from a tropical wave that crossed Central America and emerged over the eastern Pacific Ocean on 8–9 September. The wave produced disorganized showers and thunderstorms while moving westward, and a broad area of low pressure formed in association with the wave on 10 September, a couple of hundred n mi south of the coast of southern Mexico. The broad low turned northwestward while convective activity increased and gradually became better organized. Passive microwave data and satellite images indicate a well-defined, low-level circulation center formed early on 11 September, and it is estimated that a tropical depression formed by 0600 UTC that day, about 160 n mi southeast of Acapulco, Mexico. After briefly moving north-northwestward, the depression turned west-northwestward later on 11 September while moving around a low-to mid-level ridge over northern Mexico and Texas. The depression strengthened into Tropical Storm Mario by 0000 UTC 12 September, about 70 n mi southeast of Acapulco. The “best track” chart of Mario’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. The best track positions and intensities are listed in Table 11. Satellite images, surface observations, and microwave data show that the circulation of Mario was very small on 12 September while the tropical storm passed near the southwestern coast of Mexico (Fig. 4). Convection became less organized later that day due to the negative effects of moderate northeasterly shear, dry air entrainment, and the storm’s close proximity to land. The small circulation became less defined and elongated in satellite images and scatterometer data, and Mario opened into a surface trough by 0000 UTC 13 September. Some deep convection flared up along the trough axis that morning while the remnants of Mario moved westward and interacted with a mesoscale convective system that moved off the coast of west-central Mexico. By midday, scatterometer data revealed the presence of a small but well-defined area of low pressure that formed in association with the remnants of Mario. This low moved west-southwestward to westward while being steered by a ridge to the north. The associated convection was disorganized late on 13 September, but increased in coverage and organization early the following morning. Mario became a tropical depression again by 0600 UTC 14 September, then strengthened into a tropical storm 6 h later when it was located about 270 n mi south of the southern tip of the Baja California peninsula. Mario moved west-northwestward to northwestward during the next couple of days within the flow between a deep-layer ridge over northern Mexico and an upper-level trough offshore of the Baja California peninsula. ## METEOROLOGICAL STATISTICS

Observations in Mario (Figs. 2 and 3) include subjective satellite-based Dvorak technique intensity estimates from the Tropical Analysis and Forecast Branch (TAFB) and the Satellite Analysis Branch (SAB), objective Advanced Dvorak Technique (ADT) estimates and Satellite Consensus (SATCON) estimates from the Cooperative Institute for Meteorological Satellite Studies/University of Wisconsin-Madison. Data and imagery from NOAA polar-orbiting satellites including the Advanced Microwave Sounding Unit (AMSU), the NASA Global Precipitation Mission (GPM), the European Space Agency’s Advanced Scatterometer (ASCAT), the Defense Meteorological Satellite Program (DMSP) satellites, and the Time-Resolved Observations of Precipitation structure and storm Intensity with a Constellation of Smallsats (TROPICS) satellites, among others, were also useful in constructing the best track of Mario. There were no reports of sustained tropical-storm-force winds associated with Mario.

Winds and Pressure The estimated peak intensity of Mario is 55 kt from 1200–1800 UTC 15 September. This is consistent with UW-CIMSS SATCON and AiDT objective estimates during this period, as well as a 3.5/55 kt subjective Dvorak classification from TAFB at 1800 UTC that day. Due to the relatively large spread in satellite intensity estimates (45–65 kt) on 15 September and a lack of scatterometer data around the time of peak intensity, this estimate is more uncertain than usual. The estimated minimum central pressure of 994 mb is based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney (KZC) pressure-wind relationship. As Mario passed near the coast of southern Mexico, the Acapulco International Airport (MMAA) reported a peak sustained wind of 22 kt and a gust of 32 kt at 0315 UTC 12 September. A couple of days later, an automated Mexican Navy weather station on Socorro Island recorded a peak wind gust of 29 kt at 1730 UTC 14 September and a minimum sea level pressure of 1001.6 mb just before 0000 UTC 15 September as Mario passed near the island. The following day, an inland weather station at Ciudad Constitución in Baja California Sur reported a sustained wind of 21 kt and a gust of 37 kt at 1610 UTC 15 September while Mario passed well offshore.

Rainfall and Flooding Mario and its remnants contributed to an extended period of rainfall that occurred across portions of southwestern and west-central Mexico and the Baja California peninsula from 11–16 September (Fig. 6). The heaviest rainfall occurred on 12–13 September when Mario made its closest approach to the southwestern coast of Mexico, and a couple of stations in Guerrero and Colima reported more than 6 inches (~150 mm) of rainfall during the 24-h period. Media reports2 indicate some localized flooding occurred in parts of Guerrero, including Acapulco and Zihuatanejo. ## CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS

There were no direct fatalities3 associated with Mario as a tropical cyclone. Media reports4 indicate one indirect fatality occurred on 16 September in San Ignacio, Baja California Sur, where a 37-year-old man died by electrocution during flooding. There are no official damage estimates available at the time of this report. No major damage was reported in the state of Guerrero, where Mario made its closest approach to the coast of Mexico. News articles indicate that gusty winds downed some trees and power poles in Acapulco, Zihuatanejo, and surrounding areas, which resulted in brief power and communication outages. Some landslides were also reported in Acapulco. ## FORECAST AND WARNING CRITIQUE

Genesis The first genesis of Mario was not forecast well in advance (Table 2a). The incipient wave was introduced in the Tropical Weather Outlook (TWO) only 72 h prior to formation, and it reached the medium and high categories 60 h and 54 h before genesis, respectively. The disturbance was introduced with a low chance of development in the 2-day TWO 60 h before genesis. The 2-day probabilities were raised to the medium and high categories 36 h and 12 h before formation, respectively. In general, the global models did not provide a very strong signal for genesis until only a few days before Mario formed. The second genesis of Mario was not anticipated (Table 2b). The remnants of Mario were introduced with a low chance of formation in the 7-day (2-day) TWO only 18 h (12 h) prior to genesis. The 2-day probabilities did not reach the medium or high category before genesis was determined to have occurred in post analysis. Forecaster confidence for genesis was low because there were multiple vorticity centers present (including the remnants of Mario) within a broader gyre well south of Baja California. From the available model guidance, it was unclear whether these features would consolidate or remain separate, weaker features embedded within the broader circulation. Ultimately, the redevelopment that occurred was from the low-level vorticity associated with the remnants of Mario. The location of Mario’s initial genesis was accurately forecast (Fig. 7), with all of the formation areas capturing the region where Mario formed. Wind Watches and Warnings A Tropical Storm Watch was issued by the government of Mexico at 1500 UTC 12 September for the southwestern coast of Mexico from Lazaro Cardenas to Punta San Telmo. The watch was never upgraded to a warning, and it was discontinued at 0300 UTC 13 September.
