Hurricane Barbara (EP022025), 8–10 June 2025

Barbara was a category 1 hurricane (on the Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale) that remained offshore of southwestern Mexico.

Hurricane Barbara 8–10 June 2025

## BEST TRACK

The “best track2” positions and intensities for Hurricane Barbara are listed in Table 1. The “best track” chart of Barbara’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories along with available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively.

One ship reported tropical-storm-force winds in association with Barbara. The ship with call sign 9V6864 measured 35-kt winds in Barbara’s northeast quadrant on 8 June at 1800 UTC. Origin Barbara originated from a disturbance along the eastern North Pacific monsoon trough a few hundred miles south of the Gulf of Tehuantepec on 4 June. A tropical wave that emerged off the west coast of Africa on 24 May and crossed Central America on 2–3 June may have also played a role in the genesis of Barbara. The disturbance began moving west-northwestward on 5 June. Shower and thunderstorm activity started to become better organized on 7 June, and it is estimated that a tropical storm formed around 0600 UTC 8 June while centered roughly 150 n mi offshore the coast of the Mexican state of Guerrero. Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure Barbara’s peak intensity of 65 kt from 0600 UTC to 1800 UTC 9 June is based on subjective Dvorak estimates as well as UW-CIMSS objective intensity estimates. TAFB and SAB estimates both peaked at T4.0/65 kt on 9 June. In addition, the SATCON, DPRINT and DMINT algorithms all supported hurricane intensity that day, peaking in the 65 to 70 kt range. The estimated minimum central pressure of 991 mb is based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney (KZC) pressure-wind relationship.

## CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS

There were no reports of significant damage or casualties associated with Barbara.

## FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION

Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category. The genesis area was introduced in the 7-day low category well in advance of formation (over 9 days). The 7-day probability was raised to the medium category nearly 8 days prior to formation and to the high category 108 h in advance. The 2-day genesis forecasts provided sufficient lead time, reaching the medium category 60 h in advance and the high category 30 h prior to formation. Figure 4 shows composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for each category prior to the formation of Barbara. The actual genesis location fell outside and to the west-northwest of the forecast genesis ellipse areas for several of the forecasts made 7 to 9 days before genesis, because genesis occurred later than expected. Overall, 74 percent of the genesis forecasts correctly encompassed the genesis location. Of the genesis forecasts that were issued within 7 days of genesis, 97 percent correctly encompassed the genesis location. A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Barbara is given in Table 3a. Official track forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period at 12–36 h, but higher than the 5-yr mean at 48 h, albeit for a small sample size. A homogeneous comparison of the official track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b. Of note is the poor performance of the regional hurricane models HFAI, HFBI, and HWFI. The poor performance appears to be because those models showed that Tropical Storm Cosme, located southwest of Barbara (cover photo), would have a significant influence on Barbara’s track during the weakening phase, with Barbara moving westward to the north of Cosme, within Cosme’s more dominant circulation (Fig. 5). In actuality, Cosme had much less of an impact on Barbara’s track, and Barbara moved northward and northeastward after it weakened to a low-end tropical storm (Fig. 6). The aforementioned regional models were not the only models that initially indicated that Barbara would have more interaction with Cosme, and this is shown in Table 3b with the relatively large track errors by many of the models at 36 and 48 h.
