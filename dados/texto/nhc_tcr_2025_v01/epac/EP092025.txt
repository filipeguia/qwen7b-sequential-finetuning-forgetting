BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Tropical Storm Ivo are listed in Table 1. The 
best track chart of Ivo’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories along with 
available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively.  
There were no reliable reports of tropical-storm force winds associated with Ivo. 
Origin 
Ivo formed from a low-pressure area that developed along the monsoon trough southwest 
of Costa Rica on 4 August, a development likely related to a tropical wave that crossed Panama 
on 3 August. The system moved west-northwestward parallel to the coast of Central America and 
southeastern Mexico with increasing organization, and a tropical depression developed near 1200 
UTC 6 August about 295 n mi southeast of Acapulco, Mexico. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Ivo’s peak intensity of 55 kt at 1800 UTC 8 August and 0000 UTC 9 August is based on a 
blend of subjective and objective Dvorak Technique intensity estimates. It should be noted in Fig. 
2 that two scatterometer overpasses occurred near the time of the peak intensity that indicated 
lower winds. However, the scatterometer was unlikely to have resolved the maximum winds in 
Ivo’s small inner core, which was apparent in a microwave image at 1600 UTC 8 August (Fig. 4). 
The estimated minimum central pressure of 998 mb is based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney 
wind-pressure relationship for Ivo’s peak intensity and small size at that time.

CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS 
 
  
There were no reports of casualties associated with Ivo. The Meteorological Service of 
Mexico reports that Ivo produced rains of 1–3 inches along portions of the southwestern coast of 
Mexico with local amounts around 5 inches. Media reports indicate that these rains caused minor 
damage from freshwater flooding and mudslides. 
 
FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category. The formation of Ivo was generally 
well forecast, with the system being introduced into the 7-day TWO 126 h before genesis and the 
2-day TWO 54 h before genesis. Figure 5 shows composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for 
each category (low <40%, medium 40-60%, and high >60%) prior to the formation of Ivo. The 
spatial forecast generally caught the genesis location well, although the formation location was 
near the northeastern edge of the genesis forecast areas. 
A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Ivo is given in Table 3a. Official track 
forecast errors were greater than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period for the 
forecast times from 12–72 h, and less than the 5-yr mean at 96 h. A homogeneous comparison 
of the official track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b. Many of the track 
guidance models had lower average errors than the official forecast, with two of the better 
performers being the regional hurricane models HAFS-A (HFAI) and COAMPS-TC (CTCI). 
Another good performer was the experimental Google DeepMind AI model (GDMI), which had 
lower mean errors than the official forecast at all forecast periods. Examination of the individual 
official forecasts (not shown) shows that they followed the general motion of the storm well. 
However, several of the forecasts had larger errors during a period when Ivo’s low-level center 
was difficult to track and the estimated operational positions differed from the best track positions. 
A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Ivo is given in Table 4a. Official intensity 
forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period. However, the 
official forecasts had larger average errors than Climatology-Persistence (OCD5) at the 24–60 h 
forecast times, indicating these forecasts had no skill. A homogeneous comparison of the official 
intensity errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 4b. Several of the intensity 
guidance models had lower average errors than the official forecasts at most forecast times, 
including the intensity consensus (IVCN), the HFIP Correct Consensus (HCCA), and the Florida 
State Superensemble (FSSE). Examination of the individual official forecasts (not shown) 
indicates they had a consistent high bias, with Ivo not becoming as strong as forecast. 
Although Ivo was close to the coast of Mexico, no coastal watches or warnings were 
issued due to the small size of the cyclone.
