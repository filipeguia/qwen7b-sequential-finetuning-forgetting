BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Tropical Storm Dexter are listed in Table 1. 
The best track chart of Dexter’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories along 
with available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. 
There were no ship reports of winds of tropical storm force associated with Dexter.  
However, two NOAA buoys both recorded peak sustained winds of 31 kt near the system on          
3 August and were helpful in the analysis of the best track. 
Origin 
Dexter originated from a stationary front over the western Atlantic near the southeast coast 
of the United States.  A non-tropical low formed along the front around 1800 UTC 2 August 
offshore of the Carolinas, and ASCAT data that evening confirmed that the low-level wind 
circulation had become well defined.  The portion of the front that extended to the west-southwest 
of the low dissipated during the morning of 3 August as gale-force winds developed in the 
southeast quadrant.  Early that afternoon, convection quickly became better organized while the 
front that extended to the east-northeast of the low weakened.  It is estimated that the cyclone 
shed this front and became Tropical Storm Dexter around 1800 UTC 3 August about 325 n mi 
west-northwest of Bermuda. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Dexter’s 50-kt peak intensity while it was a tropical cyclone occurred from 1200–1800 UTC 
6 August and is supported by ADT, AiDT, DPRINT and SATCON intensity estimates from the 
University of Wisconsin-CIMSS, all of which peaked in the 50–55-kt range.  At the same time, 
subjective Dvorak estimates from TAFB and SAB peaked at T 3.0/45 kt.

After becoming extratropical at 0000 UTC 7 August, Dexter briefly strengthened into a 
hurricane-force extratropical cyclone early on 8 August when it was located about 410 n mi 
southeast of St. John’s, Newfoundland. 
The lowest minimum central pressure attained by Dexter while it was a tropical cyclone is 
estimated to be 999 mb, based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney (KZC) pressure-wind relationship. 
 
CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS 
 
  
There were no reports of damage or casualties associated with Dexter. 
 
FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category.  The genesis of Dexter was not well 
anticipated.  NHC first indicated a low (<40%) chance of genesis in the TWO 30 h prior to 
formation and raised the formation chances to medium 12 h prior to development.  The majority 
of the global models depicted a weak, elongated low along a frontal boundary during their runs 
between 29 July and 1 August.  On 2 August, the GFS started to consistently depict a more 
compact low in the surface fields with less frontal interaction, while the ECMWF did not show this 
until its 0000 UTC 3 August run, after the frontal low had already formed.  Figure 4 shows 
composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for each category prior to the formation of Dexter.  All 
of the TWO ellipses correctly encompassed the location where genesis occurred. 
A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Dexter is given in Table 3a.  Official track 
forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period at all time 
periods.  The NHC track errors were exceptionally low from 12–48 h, when the average track 
error for Dexter was less than half of the 5-yr means.  A homogeneous comparison of the official 
track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b.  The official forecast generally 
outperformed all available model guidance.  The HMNI and CTCI were strong performers. 
A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Dexter is given in Table 4a.  Official 
intensity forecast errors were comparable to the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period 
for the 12–36 h forecasts, and outperformed the 5-yr means at 48–60 h.  A homogeneous 
comparison of the official intensity errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 4b.  
Much of the guidance performed well, with HMNI and HFAI performing the best. 
There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Dexter.
