BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Hurricane Kiko are listed in Table 1. The best 
track chart of Kiko’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories along with 
available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. Observations also include flight-
level, stepped frequency microwave radiometer (SFMR), and dropwindsonde observations from 
five flights of the 53rd Weather Reconnaissance Squadron of the U.S. Air Force Reserve 
Command. 
The only observation of tropical-storm force winds during Kiko was from NOAA buoy 
51000, which reported sustained winds of 37 kt at 1556 UTC 9 September and a peak gust of 41 
kt as the center of Kiko passed to the southwest. 
Origin 
Kiko appears to have developed from a tropical wave that moved westward across 
Panama and Costa Rica on 22 August. This system moved westward and west-northwestward 
south of the coast of Mexico for the next several days with little development. Convection 
associated with the system first showed signs of organization on 29 August, and continued 
development led to the formation of a tropical depression at 0000 UTC 31 August about 825 n mi 
southwest of the southern tip of the Baja California Peninsula. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Kiko’s peak intensity of 125 kt early on 4 September is based on a combination of 
subjective and objective satellite intensity estimates. The minimum central pressure of 945 mb is 
based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney wind-pressure relationship for a storm of Kiko’s size and       
125-kt peak intensity.

While Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft provided data on Kiko, they did not 
investigate the cyclone until several days after the peak intensity. The maximum 700-mb flight-
level winds reported by the aircraft were 113 kt at 0601 UTC 7 September, and the minimum 
pressure reported by a dropwindsonde was 959 mb at 0604 UTC 7 September. 
 
CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS 
 
  
There were no reports of damage or casualties associated with Kiko. 
 
FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category. The genesis forecasts for Kiko were 
generally good, with the main deficiency being that the 2-day forecast genesis probabilities did 
not reach the high (>60% chance of development) category until 6 h before genesis. Another less 
skillful portion of the forecast occurred between 18-30 h before genesis, when the probabilities 
were briefly lowered as the pre-Kiko disturbance appeared to be developing more slowly than 
anticipated. Figure 4 shows composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for each category prior to 
the formation of Kiko. Spatially, the genesis of Kiko was well forecast, with the genesis location 
being near the center of the genesis forecast areas. 
A verification of official track forecasts for Kiko while it was located east of 140°W is given 
in Table 3a. Official track forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 
5-yr period at the forecast times from 12–96 h. The mean official errors were slightly above those 
for the previous 5-yr period at the 120-h forecast time. A homogeneous comparison of the official 
track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b. Many of the consensus models 
(entries TVCE through FSSE in Table 3b) have lower average errors than the official forecasts at 
most of the forecast times longer than 12 h. The best overall performance among the non-
consensus models were from the experimental Google DeepMind AI model (GDMI) and the 
HWRF regional hurricane model (HWFI). Examination of the individual forecasts (not shown) 
suggests two forecast error issues: 1) The forecasts during the initial westward motion early in 
the cyclone’s life were too far north, and 2) the forecasts during the west-northwestward motion 
in the middle part of the cyclone’s life were too far south. 
A verification of official intensity forecasts for Kiko while it was located east of 140°W is 
given in Table 4a. Official intensity forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the 
previous 5-yr period for the 24-72 h forecast times, and greater than the 5-yr means at the other 
times. A homogeneous comparison of the official intensity errors with selected guidance models 
is given in Table 4b. The official forecast had lower mean errors than the vast majority of the 
intensity guidance, with only the HFIP Corrected Consensus model (HCCA) systematically having 
errors less than or close to the official forecasts. Examination of the individual forecasts (not 
shown) showed two significant error mechanisms. First, Kiko intensified more than forecast during

its rapid intensification to peak intensity.  Second, near the end of the cyclone’s life, Kiko 
weakened faster than forecast. 
For the period when Kiko was west of 140°W, a verification of the official track forecasts

While Kiko passed within 60-70 n mi to the northeast of the western Hawaiian Islands, 
there were no coastal watches or warnings issued for the cyclone. 
 
IMPACT-BASED DECISION SUPPORT SERVICES (IDSS) AND 
PUBLIC COMMUNICATION 
 
The NHC began formal communication with emergency managers on Friday, September 
5 as Kiko approached the Central Pacific basin. A decision support briefing was provided to 
emergency managers and coordinated through the FEMA Hurricane Liaison Team embedded at 
the NHC. The briefing was a federal video-teleconference with FEMA HQ and FEMA Region 9.
