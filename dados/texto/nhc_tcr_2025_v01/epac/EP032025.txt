BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Tropical Storm Cosme are listed in Table 1. 
The “best track” chart of Cosme’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories 
along with available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. There were no credible 
ship observations of tropical-storm-force winds associated with Cosme. 
Origin 
Cosme originated from a broad area of low pressure located within the eastern North 
Pacific monsoon trough about 500 n mi offshore of southwestern Mexico on 5 June. A tropical 
wave that emerged from the west coast of Africa on 24 May and crossed Central America on 2–
3 June may have also contributed to its genesis. Showers and thunderstorms associated with this 
system gradually became better organized as it began to lift north-northwestward on 6–7 June.  
The system is estimated to have become a tropical depression at 0600 UTC 8 June, when deep 
convection consolidated over the low-level circulation center, with curved bands wrapping into the 
center from the west and southern semicircles. The cyclone strengthened into a 35-kt tropical 
storm about 6 h later. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Cosme’s estimated peak intensity of 60 kt from 1800 UTC 9 June to 1200 UTC 10 June 
is supported by subjective Dvorak estimates from TAFB and SAB, along with objective estimates 
from UW-CIMSS. Ultra-high-resolution ASCAT data during this period (Fig. 4) showed peak winds 
of 45–50 kt, while an RCM-1 synthetic aperture radar (SAR) pass from a few hours earlier 
indicated maximum winds of 63 kt in the southeastern quadrant. Although offset by a few hours, 
the two passes revealed a similar wind structure. However, it is worth noting that SAR wind data

FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category. NHC introduced the feature in the 
TWO in the low category (<30%) in both the 2- and 7-day periods, 54 and 60 h before genesis, 
respectively, although the 7-day outlook provided much shorter-than-average lead time. This 
reduced lead time was partly due to the proximity of Tropical Storm Barbara, which also formed 
several hundred miles to the northeast of Cosme at 0600 UTC 8 June. Its presence added 
uncertainty in the numerical guidance, as the models struggled to depict both systems until shortly 
before formation. Probabilities reached the high category (>60%) within 24 hours of the system’s 
initial inclusion in the TWO. Fig. 5 shows composites of 7-day TWO genesis areas for each 
category prior to Cosme’s formation. While the genesis location was captured within all of the 
outlook areas, it ultimately occurred near the southern edge of most of the regions depicted. 
A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Cosme is given in Table 3a. Official track 
forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-year period at 12, 24, 
and 60 hours. At 36 and 48 hours, the forecast errors were higher than the 5-year means, although 
the small sample size limits meaningful interpretation. A homogeneous comparison of the official 
track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b.  
A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Cosme is given in Table 4a. Official 
intensity forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-year period at 
all forecast times through 60 hours. A homogeneous comparison of the official intensity errors 
with selected guidance models is given in Table 4b. 
There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Cosme.
