BEST TRACK 
 
The “best track2” positions and intensities for Tropical Storm Lorenzo are listed in Table 
1. The best track chart of Lorenzo’s path is given in Fig. 1, with the wind and pressure histories 
along with available observations3 shown in Figs. 2 and 3, respectively. 
There were no surface reports of tropical-storm-force winds from Lorenzo. 
Origin 
Lorenzo formed from a low-latitude tropical wave that moved westward from the coast of 
Africa on 9 October. The system first showed signs of convective organization on 11 October, 
and the next day it developed an area of 35-kt winds to the northeast of a vorticity center 
embedded within a sharp trough.  At about 0600 UTC 13 October, a well-defined circulation 
developed accompanied by organized convection, and it is estimated that Tropical Storm Lorenzo 
formed at that time about 900 n mi west of the Cabo Verde Islands. 
Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure 
Lorenzo’s peak intensity of 50 kt is supported by multiple ASCAT overpasses showing 
maximum vectors of around 45 kt near 0000 UTC 14 October, along with the likelihood that the 
maximum winds were undersampled. 
The estimated minimum central pressure of 1000 mb is based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney 
wind-pressure relationship for a storm of Lorenzo’s intensity and size.

CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS 
 
  
There were no reports of damage or casualties associated with Lorenzo. 
FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION 
 
Table 2 provides the number of hours in advance of formation with the first NHC Tropical 
Weather Outlook (TWO) forecast in each likelihood category. Temporally, the development of 
Lorenzo was not well anticipated, with the system not being introduced into the TWO until 48 h 
before genesis and the system not being given a high probability of development in the 7-day 
outlook until the last 6 h before genesis occurred. Figure 4 shows composites of 7-day TWO 
genesis areas for each category prior to the formation of Lorenzo. While the number of genesis 
forecasts is low, the genesis location was consistently inside the forecast areas. 
A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Lorenzo is given in Table 3a. Official track 
forecast errors were greater than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period at the 12- 
and 24-h forecast times, but were less than the 5-yr mean errors at the 36- and 48-h forecast 
times. It should be noted that Lorenzo was only a tropical cyclone for 54 h, so the number of 
forecasts is small. A homogeneous comparison of the official track errors with selected guidance 
models is given in Table 3b. Many of the track guidance models had lower average errors than 
the official forecast, but again the number of forecasts was small and results are not very 
meaningful. 
A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Lorenzo is given in Table 4a. Official 
intensity forecast errors were greater than the mean official errors for the previous 5-yr period. A 
homogeneous comparison of the official intensity errors with selected guidance models is given 
in Table 4b. Many of the guidance models had lower average errors than the official forecast at 
the 24-, 36-, and 48-h forecast times. Examination of the individual forecasts (not shown) indicates 
that the official intensity forecasts were too low on 13 October and then too high on 14–15 October 
since Lorenzo weakened faster than originally forecast. 
There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Lorenzo.
