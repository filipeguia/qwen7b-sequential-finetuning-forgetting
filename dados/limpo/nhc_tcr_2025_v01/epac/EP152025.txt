Hurricane Octave (EP152025), 30 September–9 October 2025

Hurricane Octave 30 SEPTEMBER–9 OCTOBER 2025

## BEST TRACK

Hurricane Octave took a rather unusual track across the eastern Pacific as the cyclone was highly influenced by two other tropical cyclones, Hurricane Priscilla and Tropical Storm Raymond (Fig. 1). After tracking steadily west-northwestward for several days, Octave made an abrupt U-turn on 5 October. The cyclone was steered eastward beginning that day by an approaching upper-level trough to the northwest along with the breakdown of a subtropical ridge to the east of the cyclone, caused by the large circulation of Hurricane Priscilla. Octave was then influenced by the circulations of both Priscilla and Raymond during the next several days, as it tracked generally eastward and eventually dissipated by 10 October. The “best track2” positions and intensities for Hurricane Octave are listed in Table 1. The best track chart of Octave’s path is given in Fig. 2, with the wind and pressure histories along with available observations3 shown in Figs. 3 and 4, respectively. There were no surface observations of winds of tropical storm force associated with Octave. Origin The origin of Octave can be traced to a convective complex that developed over the higher terrain of the far northern portion of South America on 16–17 September, fueled by an influx of deeper moisture feeding in off the tropical Atlantic from the east. The disturbance moved westward across Venezuela and Colombia between 17 and 19 September before emerging off the Colombian coast on 20 September. The disturbance then crossed Panama and Costa Rica on 21–22 September and moved offshore of Central America by 23 September. The disturbance continued steadily west-southwestward over the following several days, and a small area of low pressure developed several hundred miles off the southwestern coast of Mexico on 29 September. Convection gradually increased and became better organized, and the system became a tropical depression by 0000 UTC 30 September, about 825 n mi south of the southern tip of the Baja California Peninsula. Peak Intensity and Minimum Pressure Octave’s peak intensity of 80 kt is based on a subjective Dvorak current intensity estimate of T4.5 from SAB, as well as objective intensity estimates from UW-CIMSS that ranged from 75 to 79 kt at 0000 UTC 6 October. An internal subjective Dvorak analysis using the eye pattern technique yielded a data T-number of 5.0 for roughly half of the 10-minute GOES-18 satellite images between 2100 UTC 5 October and 0000 UTC 6 October. All of these data were considered when assigning the peak intensity for Octave. A 0209 UTC 6 October Radarsat Constellation Mission-2 (RCM-2) Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) pass indicated a well-defined structure to the cyclone, with peak winds in the northwest quadrant of Octave’s circulation (Fig.5). The estimated minimum central pressure of 980 mb is based on the Knaff-Zehr-Courtney pressure-wind relationship for a storm of Octave’s intensity and size.

## CASUALTY AND DAMAGE STATISTICS

There were no reports of damage or casualties associated with Octave.

## FORECAST AND WARNING VERIFICATION

The genesis forecasts for Octave’s formation were poor, likely a result of the global models struggling to resolve what would eventually be three tropical cyclones in close proximity to each other off the western coast of Mexico (Fig. 1). The disturbance from which Octave developed was first highlighted in the Tropical Weather Outlook (TWO) 60 h prior to formation (Table 2). The 7-day genesis probability was raised to the medium category 42 h before formation and to the high category 30 h in advance. Two-day genesis forecasts were a little better; low probabilities were introduced 60 h prior to formation, medium probabilities 30 h prior, and high probabilities 18 h before development occurred. Octave’s genesis location was on the south side of all genesis formation regions. However, the system formed within each of the genesis forecast areas depicted in NHC’s Graphical TWO, resulting in a 100% hit rate (Fig. 6). A verification of NHC official track forecasts for Octave is given in Table 3a. Official track forecast errors were lower than the 5-year means at all forecast intervals. A homogeneous comparison of the official track errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 3b. The official track forecasts outperformed nearly all guidance from 12 h through 72 h. Several models outperformed the official track forecasts at 96 h and 120 h. Notably, the Google DeepMind Ensemble Mean (GDMI) outperformed the official track forecasts at all forecast hours, except the 12-h interval.

A verification of NHC official intensity forecasts for Octave is given in Table 4a. Official intensity forecast errors were lower than the mean official errors for the previous 5-year period at all forecast intervals. A homogeneous comparison of the official intensity errors with selected guidance models is given in Table 4b. The official intensity forecasts outperformed nearly all of the intensity guidance. The exception again was the GDMI, which outperformed the official intensity forecasts at many forecast intervals. There were no coastal watches or warnings issued for Octave.
